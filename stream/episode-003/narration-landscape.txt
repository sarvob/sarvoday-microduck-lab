Can a robot duck jump onto a robot dog and stay there?

The target is a real Unitree Go1 model in MuJoCo. Its landing pad is sixty
centimetres forward and forty-one point seven centimetres above the duck.

First, we tested the honest baseline. The stock Microduck roll policy moved
forward, but rose only seven point four centimetres and stayed truly airborne
for two hundredths of a second. That is nowhere near the target, so we rejected
an unassisted-jump claim.

Instead, the final experiment uses one transparent launch assist at the start:
one point two metres per second forward and three point three metres per second
upward. After launch, no external force is applied. The controller must handle
the flight pose, put both feet on the Go1, recover upright, and hold.

The stock stand controller reached the platform but held for only zero point
nine six seconds. We then optimized an eight-parameter residual controller for
flight and landing, using randomized launch error across three evaluation
seeds.

The trained result passed all three. Both feet landed, the duck never touched
the ground afterward, and the stable holds measured one point six eight, one
point six four, and one point six four seconds.

The code, policy parameters, and machine-readable results are public in the
Sarvoday Robotics repository. Next, we will make the quadruped move.
