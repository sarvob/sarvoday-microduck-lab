# Episode 006 v2 — Could This Tiny Duck Become a Goalkeeper?

## Audience story

Lead with the emotional shape of a sports challenge, not the controller. An
original cinematic keeper dive establishes the standard. Microduck then gets a
two-hour-sized control budget and three visible disadvantages: it cannot
sidestep, its head camera can lose the ball while turning, and its body covers
only a small part of the goal.

The awkward pivot must be explained early. It is genuine locomotion behavior,
not hidden or edited away. Keep the main canvas on the robot and ball. Put
coordinates, speed, prediction gain, and shot counters inside a narrow right
rail that never competes with the action.

## Edit rhythm

- 0:00–0:18 original cinematic human-goalkeeper B-roll, whistle, crowd rise.
- 0:18–0:45 hard cut to Microduck's hardest miss and the challenge.
- 0:45–1:35 reveal the three constraints with motion, not a lecture card.
- 1:35–2:20 let the baseline look competent before the far-post failure.
- 2:20–3:15 reveal one visual idea: predict the crossing point.
- 3:15–4:05 fast six-gain training montage and ticking clock.
- 4:05–5:00 fifteen-shot final test with crowd energy and a visible counter.
- 5:00–5:18 result, applause, next challenge.

## Integrity

- Human goalkeeper B-roll is an original low-poly Blender animation, not
  broadcast or celebrity footage.
- All Microduck and ball motion remains measured MuJoCo evidence.
- Narration may heighten pacing but may not invent results or fake contact.
- The search is described as “small enough to fit inside a couple of hours,”
  not as a claim about a recorded wall-clock deadline.
- No personal name, email, or unrelated project appears.
