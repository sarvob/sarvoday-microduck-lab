Before we finish, here is a practical way to inspect a controller handoff. Watch
three signals together: body rotation, projected gravity, and displacement.
During the successful zero point eight two second run, the roll policy carries
the trunk through inversion, then the standing policy receives a pose it can
recover. The motion damps instead of accelerating into another turn.

Now compare the zero point eight four second run. The difference is only one
fifty-hertz control step, but the trunk has already crossed the useful capture
region. The standing controller begins from the wrong angular state, so it
cannot remove momentum before the body rotates again.

This is why we keep the failed boundary case. A polished success clip shows
what happened; a nearby failure helps explain why. When reproducing this work,
keep the policies frozen, use the published seeds, verify all four physical
gates, and report the first failing candidate beside the selected one. That
makes the result easier to audit, teach, and improve.
